The present experiment aims to investigate how the strategic environment—whether interactions involve strategic substitutes or complements—and the availability of communication influence cooperation levels in repeated two-person games. The study involved 174 subjects, paired in fixed pairs throughout 30 periods, enabling an analysis of how communication and strategic context jointly affect behavior over time.

In each period, participants simultaneously chose actions, with payoffs determined by the chosen strategies according to the predefined payoff matrix. The experiment included two treatment conditions: one in which participants could freely communicate via an online chat box for one minute before making their decisions each period, and a control condition without communication. The study sought to examine whether communication facilitates cooperation differently depending on whether the game’s strategic environment involves substitution or complementarity, and how this interaction evolves across multiple periods. Additionally, the experiment aimed to analyze the content of the chat messages to understand how verbal communication reflects, influences, or facilitates strategic reasoning and cooperation within these different environments.
